01Admission

An account only enters the system with a named, dated, sourced reason to buy, or because it sits in a priority market. Everything else is dropped. That keeps the pipeline small enough to work properly.

02Selection and ranking

Admitted accounts are ranked on how fresh the signal is, how well it fits a service line and whether any exclusion rule applies. Past outcomes nudge the ranking, but they never decide on their own. The top few each day become the working list.

03Research per account

An agent builds a research packet for each account. Before anything is drafted, the packet is challenged by an analyst whose job is to confirm or reject the agent's assumptions. Read more about that loop here.

04Agent orchestration

Work is routed by what it needs. Cheaper models handle bulk and mechanical steps, stronger models handle judgment, and a supervisor reviews what comes back. Every planning step ends in a state a smaller model can pick up and run.

05How the message is written

Messages are built from published proof, never from internal claims. They are shaped by persona and follow a defined sequence of touches. Quality gates check each draft, and a missing step stops the run. Nothing is sent without my approval.

06Connected to a central intelligence layer

The daily queue isn't built from scratch. It draws on Sentinel, the signal layer that watches the market and decides what is hot. Read more about this engine here.

Built to be token-efficient

  • A local ledger is the working memory, so agents read one compact file instead of querying the CRM over and over.
  • Bulk reading and extraction go to cheaper models, and the strongest model is kept for judgment.
  • Each run starts lean: only the instructions and tools the job needs are loaded.
